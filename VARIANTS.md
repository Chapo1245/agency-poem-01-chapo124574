# Candidate variants — all original, written fresh for this entry

Self-graded against the judge rubric, in order:
(1) is a poem about agency, (2) moves the reader, (3) insight,
(4) craft, (5) freshness.

## A — "The Wheel" (CHOSEN, 479 chars)

I gave the wheel to hands that never tire,
to eyes that never blink, to a mind that never dreams.
It took the long straight miles while I watched the fields go by,
and for a while it felt like rest —

until the road bent, and I reached for a wheel that wasn't there.
My fingers closed on air. My palms remembered steering
before my mind remembered why.

Agency is not the driving.
It is the reaching.
The moment your hand closes on nothing,
you learn exactly what you gave away.

**Self-grade:** (1) squarely on-subject — handing over agency, then reaching back.
(2) Strong turn at "until the road bent"; "My fingers closed on air" lands.
(3) Best insight of the three: agency is the reaching, not the driving — a genuine
reframing. (4) Tight; "My palms remembered steering before my mind remembered why"
is the best single line. (5) Wheel imagery is familiar, but the "reaching" reframing
is fresh. Chosen because its insight line is the most memorable and its ending
lands hardest.

## B — "The Long Way Home" (runner-up, 526 chars)

The map knows the fastest road. It always has.
It knows the traffic, the tolls, the time saved to the minute.

Tonight I took the long way home on purpose —
past the bakery that closes at nine, past the river
where the light comes off the water in pieces,
past the house with the blue door I have never knocked on.

I arrived late. I arrived wrong by every measure the map keeps.
And it was mine. Every wasted minute was mine.

That is the whole of it: to choose badly, and to know it,
and to call the bad choice yours anyway.

**Self-grade:** (1) on-subject. (2) Vivid detour images; "Every wasted minute was
mine" lands. (3) Strong insight (the right to choose badly) but the final stanza
tips into lecture, which the rubric penalises. (4) Good concrete craft.
(5) Fresh conceit. Runner-up.

## C — "The Baton" (third, 521 chars)

Agency is a baton, not a crown.
It is passed in the open palm, mid-stride, at full speed —
and the race is won or lost in the half-second of the exchange.

Hold it too long and your arm goes numb;
you run alone, exhausted, certain you are in control
of a race you are losing.

Let go too soon and it clatters on the track;
someone else picks it up, and the running
was never really yours.

The art is the handoff: the grip, the glance,
the moment two hands hold the same thing at once
and neither one is afraid to let go.

**Self-grade:** (1) on-subject. (2) Vivid opening, but the ending is the softest of
the three. (3) "A baton, not a crown" opens well, but the poem then elaborates the
metaphor rather than deepening insight; the middle is didactic. (4) Tight lines.
(5) The baton metaphor is the most familiar of the three. Third.
